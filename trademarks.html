Why speed matters

Most trademark registers reward whoever files first, not whoever used the name first.

In the UK, the EU, and most jurisdictions worldwide, trademark rights are generally granted on a first-to-file basis, not first-to-use. A name you've been operating under for years isn't automatically yours in the eyes of the register. If someone else files for it first, in a class that covers what you do, they can hold the legal right to it, and you can be the one asked to stop using your own name.

This shows up most in fast-moving sectors like iGaming and crypto, where a live, recognizable brand is worth copying and a competitor, an opportunist, or an unrelated party filing in another jurisdiction can move faster than you'd expect. Filing early isn't caution. It's the only way to make sure the name stays yours.

What actually gets protected first

Registration itself isn't instant, most jurisdictions take somewhere between 4 and 18 months to reach full registration. But the filing date is what counts: it's protected from the day you file, even while examination is still running.

That's why waiting until "things are more settled" is the wrong instinct. What you're securing is the date, not the certificate.

What to expect

The clock starts on the day you file, not the day you're registered.

Timelines vary by register and whether anyone objects, but the shape is broadly the same everywhere.

Day 0

Filed

Your filing date is what's protected from this point, even while examination is still ongoing.

Month 2 to 3

Published

The application is published on the register, opening a window for third parties to raise an objection.

Month 3 to 4

Opposition window

If no one objects in this window, the application moves forward to registration.

Month 4 to 18

Registered

Certificate issued. Valid for 10 years, and renewable indefinitely after that.

Where to file

Register where you're actually licensed and operating, not just where it's simplest to file.

The right jurisdiction depends on where your licence sits and where your players are, not a default. These are the markets we see iGaming and crypto brands need most often.
