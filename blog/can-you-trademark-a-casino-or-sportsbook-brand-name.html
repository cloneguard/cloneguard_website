Can You Trademark a Casino or Sportsbook Brand Name?

CloneGuard Team 13 September 2026 4 min read

Yes. Casino and sportsbook names are registered as trademarks every day, in the UK, the EU, and most of the world, and it's one of the highest-leverage things an operator can do early: a registered mark is what turns a takedown request from a favour a platform does you into something they're legally obligated to act on. The two things that actually decide the outcome are which markets you file in and whether the name itself is strong enough to register and defend.

Where it's straightforward

In the UK and EU, a distinctive casino or sportsbook name gets registered as a normal matter of course. The trademark office isn't weighing in on whether gambling is lawful, it's checking that the name is distinctive and doesn't clash with something already registered. Licensing is a separate conversation with the gambling regulator, not the trademark office. If your primary markets are the UK and EU, this is a fast, predictable process.

The one market that plays by different rules

The US treats real-money gambling brands differently to almost everything else. Federal registration through the USPTO carries lawful-use-in-commerce restrictions that don't apply to most Crypto and fintech brands, so a name can register cleanly in the UK and EU and still get refused federally in the US, for reasons that have nothing to do with how strong the name is. This isn't a reason to skip the US, it's a reason to check it as its own market before you file there rather than assuming one global filing covers you everywhere. Knowing this upfront is a real advantage: it's the difference between planning your filing strategy around it and finding out the hard way after a refusal.

What actually gets a strong name refused

Two things kill most applications, and both are avoidable if you check first:

  • A name that's too generic. Combinations built entirely from words like "bet," "casino," or "spin" with nothing distinctive added either get refused outright or register so weakly they're hard to enforce against a near-identical clone later. The names that hold up are the ones with a real distinctive core, not just a descriptive label on the industry.
  • Someone already got there first. A confusingly similar mark already on the register in your class blocks or opposes a new filing. This is the single most avoidable failure, because it's fully checkable before you spend a marketing budget on the name.

What's actually being registered

Filings sit in Class 41 (gambling and entertainment services) for most iGaming brands, with a second filing in Class 36 (financial services) if the brand also handles deposits, payouts, or crypto-denominated wagers directly under its own name. Getting the class right matters less as a filing formality and more because it's what a registration actually gives you standing to enforce later, a mark registered only for "entertainment services" is weaker ground to stand on against a clone that's specifically impersonating your payment flow.

Bottom line

File where you operate, check the name and the US market before you commit marketing spend to either, and treat the class as part of your enforcement strategy, not paperwork. This is general guidance, not a substitute for advice on your specific brand, which is handled by specialist legal partners once you're ready to file.

Frequently asked

Can you trademark a casino or sportsbook brand name?

Yes. It's a normal registration in the UK and EU, filed in Class 41 for gambling and entertainment services, with Class 36 added if the brand handles its own deposits or payouts. The US is the market to check separately, since it has its own registrability rules for real-money gambling.

Can I trademark a real-money gambling brand in the US?

Sometimes, and it's worth confirming before you file there. Real-money gambling faces federal lawful-use-in-commerce restrictions at the USPTO that most Crypto and fintech brands don't run into. A name that registers cleanly in the UK and EU can still be refused in the US for reasons unrelated to the name's strength, so treat it as its own market rather than an automatic extension of a UK or EU filing.

Why would a casino or sportsbook name get refused?

Almost always one of two reasons: the name is too generic to be distinctive (plain combinations of words like "bet" or "casino"), or a confusingly similar mark is already registered in the same class. Both are checkable before you file, which is why a pre-filing search is worth doing on every name you're seriously considering.
