Can You Trademark a Casino or Sportsbook Brand Name?

CloneGuard Team 13 September 2026 4 min read

Usually, yes. Casino and sportsbook names get registered as trademarks every day. The part operators tend to get wrong isn't whether it's possible, it's which class to file in, which market to start with, and whether the name they've already committed to marketing is actually registrable at all. Getting that wrong doesn't just cost a filing fee, it can mean discovering the problem after the brand is already live.

What class actually covers a casino or sportsbook

Most iGaming brands file in Class 41, which covers gambling and entertainment services. That's the class that protects the name itself, in connection with running a casino, sportsbook, or betting platform.

It's not automatically the only class you need. If your brand also handles deposits, withdrawals, or crypto-denominated wagers directly under its own name, that can fall under Class 36, financial services, which Class 41 doesn't cover. Operators who only file in one class sometimes find a name registered for "entertainment services" doesn't give them standing to act on a clone site that's specifically impersonating their payment flow, because the registration never covered that activity in the first place.

Where registration is straightforward

In the UK and EU, a distinctive casino or sportsbook name in Class 41 is a normal registration. The trademark office isn't assessing whether gambling itself is lawful, it's assessing whether the mark is distinctive and doesn't conflict with an existing one. Licensing is a separate question handled by the gambling regulator, not the trademark office.

Where it gets complicated

The US is the market where this trips people up. Real-money gambling faces registrability restrictions at the USPTO that don't apply to most Crypto and fintech brands, tied to federal lawful-use-in-commerce requirements rather than state-by-state licensing. A name can be perfectly registrable in the UK and EU and still get refused federally in the US, for reasons that have nothing to do with how distinctive the name is. This is worth checking before filing, not after the fee is paid, since a refusal on these grounds isn't something a stronger name or a re-submission fixes.

What gets a name refused anywhere

Two reasons account for most refusals, in any jurisdiction:

  • Merely descriptive marks. A name that's just a generic combination of words like "bet," "casino," or "spin" with little else distinctive gives the trademark office grounds to refuse it outright, or to grant a registration so weak it's difficult to enforce against a near-identical clone later.
  • Prior conflicting marks. If a confusingly similar name is already registered in the same class, a new application gets refused or opposed. This is checkable in advance, and it's the single most avoidable reason a filing fails.

The practical approach

Check registrability and conflicts before filing, not after. File in the classes that actually cover what the brand does, not just the obvious one. And treat US filings for real-money gambling brands as a market-specific question, not an assumption that what works in the UK and EU will work everywhere. None of this is exhaustive legal advice, and specifics get handled by specialist legal partners as part of an actual filing, but it's the shape of the decision before you commit to a name.

Frequently asked

Which trademark class covers a casino or sportsbook brand?

Most iGaming brands file in Class 41, which covers gambling and entertainment services. Brands that also handle deposits, payouts, or crypto-denominated wagers often file a second application in Class 36 for financial services, since the two aren't automatically covered by one filing.

Can I trademark a real-money gambling brand in the US?

It depends on the market. Real-money gambling faces registrability restrictions at the USPTO that don't apply to most Crypto and fintech brands, tied to federal lawful-use requirements rather than state licensing. It's worth checking before filing, not after you've paid the fee.

Why would a casino or sportsbook name get refused?

The two most common reasons are a name judged merely descriptive of the service itself (generic combinations of words like "bet" or "casino" with little else distinctive), and a prior conflicting mark already on the register in the same class. Both are checkable before you file.
